# Spike #60: product icons from Iconify

**Question:** how do we give every product a clean, consistent icon from Iconify to replace the initial-letter tile?
Which icon set, how does each product get its icon, how are icons served, and what licences apply?

**Answer (recommendation):** use **Fluent Emoji Flat**. Pick each product's icon with **Jev**, choosing from that set's
own **Food & Drink category** (136 icons) plus about 10 extras, once per product on the shelf-life timer, as with shelf
life. When Jev's confidence is **below 0.5**, use the product's **aisle icon** instead (from the aisles feature, #71),
and the letter tile only until aisles exist. **Bundle** the curated icons as static files in the UI and store only the
icon **name** on the product. No attribution is needed on screen. Gary can change any icon from a small picker in
Edit details.

- **Gallery for review** (icons in the real 44 px tile, both themes): https://claude.ai/artifact/QF4L4h1s6As9Nx83nwob6S
- **Script:** `docs/spikes/60-iconify-icons.py` (reads the Jev key from `JEV_API_KEY`; product names go only to Jev; nothing
  changes live data).

## Sample

55 products: the **47 known products in live stock** on 2026-10-18 (exported read-only with `deploy/verify/cosmos.sh`),
11 of them loose (leeks, squash, salad leaves, ham hock terrine...), plus **8 typical extras** marked * to cover kinds
the house didn't have that day (bananas, a sourdough loaf, eggs, butter, frozen peas, fish fingers, rice, baked beans).
It includes odd ones: a pre-workout powder, a sweetener, a spice rub and a test product.

## Approaches compared

1. **Search per product:** search the set for the cleaned name, then the most specific Open Food Facts category, then
   single words, and take the first hit.
2. **Search, then Jev picks:** the same searches give up to 255 candidates, and a Jev `choice` picks one by icon **name**.
3. **Curated food set + Jev:** a Jev `choice` over the set's own food category: Fluent Emoji Flat's "Food & Drink"
   (136 icons) or IconPark Outline's "Foods" (121). The set's own category makes the curation reproducible, with no
   hand-picking.
4. **Aisle icon as the floor:** a Jev `choice` of one of #71's 14 draft aisles, each with one Fluent icon (Fruit & veg →
   leafy green, Tins & jars → canned food, ...).

Jev gets the same product state as the shelf-life questions (name, brand, size, categories, usual storage). The
instruction is: pick the icon that best shows what the product is, by the kind of food, not the brand.

### Hit rate (my judgement, for Gary to check in the gallery)

| Approach | Right | Close | Wrong | No icon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Search, Fluent (Noto picks the same names) | 19 | ~7 | ~16 | 13 |
| 2 Search + Jev, Fluent | 28 | ~8 | ~6 | 13 |
| **3 Food set + Jev, Fluent** | **39** | **8** | **8** | 0 |
| 3 Food set + Jev, IconPark | ~36 | ~14 | ~5 | 0 |
| 4 Aisle only | a generic floor: right aisle for 53 of 55 (only naan and the topokki pot are wrong, both put in Frozen) | | | |

"Close" means a reasonable stand-in (onion for leeks, honey pot for maple syrup, peanuts for walnuts, pouring liquid
for gravy). "Wrong" means it would puzzle you (melon for squash, salt for sweetener, fried shrimp for fish fingers).

**Confidence is useful here.** In approach 3 (Fluent), every pick below 0.5 confidence was close or wrong (maple syrup
0.49, chia 0.34, pre-workout 0.46, soured cream 0.34, kuri squash 0.43, fish fingers 0.40), and none was right. So
gating at 0.5 and using the aisle icon instead turns 4 wrong icons into sensible generic ones without losing a right one.
That leaves **39 right, 6 close, 4 wrong and 6 aisle icons**, so about 9 in 10 tiles would look right.

**Why search loses:** the set's names are emoji names, so search is literal. "Granular low calorie sweetener" finds
**low-battery**, "Rich Italian Coffee Bags" finds **clutch-bag**, "Rice Krispie Squares" finds **blue-square**, "White
Sourdough Loaf" finds **white-cane**, and "cherry tomatoes" finds **cherries**. Nothing at all for gravy, mustard, mayo,
lasagne, leeks or ham. Jev understands the product, which is the point. Approach 2 fixes the misfires only when search
happened to return the right candidate.

**The remaining misses** are gaps in the set, not in Jev. Fluent has no pumpkin or squash, no seeds and no supplements,
and its fish is filed under Animals, not food. Adding about 10 icons from outside the food category fixes the clear
ones: `fish`, `seedling` (seeds), `herb`, `sheaf-of-rice`, `jack-o-lantern` (squash, at a pinch), `pill`
(supplements), `ice`, `cow`, `chicken`, `pig-face`.

**Recommended: Fluent Emoji Flat.**
- It has the best food coverage of these sets, and full colour makes products easy to tell apart at a glance.
- Its flat style sits calmly on the tile's green tint (`#E4F0E9`), as the tile mock shows.
- Noto has the same coverage, because both are Unicode emoji with the same names (approach 1 picked identical names
  for every product), but its glossier style is busier at 28 px.
- IconPark in accent green matches the current look exactly, but it's generic ("vegetables" for 6 different products),
  so rows look alike.
- Tabler doesn't have the food coverage.

**Licences:** all four allow use in our app with no on-screen credit. MIT and Apache 2.0 only require that the licence
text travels with the copies, so the follow-up adds a `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` in `FE/` (and the bundled icon folder)
with Fluent's MIT text. Nothing goes next to the Open Food Facts credit.

## Serving

| Option | Privacy | Availability | Phone | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iconify public API at runtime (`@iconify/react` fetching icons) | Sends icon names, not product names, to a third party | Depends on api.iconify.design. **This spike was blocked by it:** a burst of a few hundred searches got HTTP 429 with `Retry-After: 1917` (32 minutes) | Fetches per icon; cached | No |
| **Bundle the curated icons as static files** in the UI build (`/icons/<name>.svg`, ~146 files, ~180 KB raw before compression, each 1–2 KB), store only the **name** on the product | Nothing leaves our site | Same as the site | Only the icons on screen load, cached by the browser | **Yes** |
| Store the SVG on the product document | Fine | Fine | Bigger stock-list responses (1–2 KB × every product, every time) | No: duplicates the same SVG across products, and harder to restyle |

Self-hosting the Iconify API isn't worth it: we need ~146 fixed icons, not search. The icon files come from the
`@iconify-json/fluent-emoji-flat` npm package (as this spike used), checked into `FE/public/icons/` by a small script,
so the build has no runtime dependency. The whole set is 9 MB, so only the curated icons are copied.

## Also answered

- **When an icon is chosen:** once per product, by the **existing shelf-life timer** (every 5 minutes, 20 products a run).
  It picks known products with no `icon` yet, in the same Jev request as the shelf-life or aisle questions where they're
  asked together (`docs/spikes/40-jev.md`; #65 shows several questions in one request). **No separate backfill:** the
  timer picks up existing products itself over the first runs. Unknown items (no name yet) get none until they're named.
- **Data model** (product document): `icon` (the name, e.g. `bread`, from the curated list), `iconSource` (`jev` |
  `manual`), `iconConfidence` and `iconEstimatedAt`. When confidence is below 0.5, `icon` stays empty and the UI uses
  the aisle icon. A **manual** icon (`iconSource: manual`) is never overwritten by Jev; an ETag-guarded replace, as for
  shelf life. If the curated list ever drops an icon, the UI falls back to the aisle icon, then the letter tile.
- **Picture priority** (the card's decision): the product's **own photo** (when photo identification stores one, not yet)
  → its **icon** → its **aisle icon** (after #71) → the **letter tile**.
- **Gary's control:** an **Icon** button in Edit details opens a grid of the curated icons with a search box, filtering by
  name (e.g. "bre" → bread, baguette, flatbread). Saving sets `iconSource: manual`. **Effort: small**, about half a day: the
  grid is the bundled list, the search is client-side, and one `PUT /api/products/{id}/icon` endpoint.
- **The digest email:** keep it **text only**. Email clients block or mangle SVG, and image files would need hosting and
  downloading. The emoji characters behind Fluent's icons would work in email (🍞 🧀), but the set's data has no
